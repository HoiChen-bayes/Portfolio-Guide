# Point-in-time market evidence: calendar April–June 2026

This is a retrospective reconstruction executed on the timestamp recorded in `data/research/market_evidence.json`. It is not a forecast authored in March or May. The packet contains ten evidence records for P&G and ten for Travelers, each separating the published observation from an explicitly discretionary scenario adjustment. The repaired register admits nineteen records and excludes Marsh TRV06 pending resolution of conflicting publication dates. Short quotations are accompanied by located source fact tables; the initial partial-support version and failed model audits remain preserved. The suggested ranges are uncalibrated analyst judgments, not measured elasticities, official budgets, probabilities or final forecasts.

## Publication boundaries and revision control

The pre-quarter cutoff is March 31, 2026. P&G's January 22 report and Travelers' January 21 report are eligible; their April 24 and April 16 reports are not. The mid-quarter cutoff is May 15, 2026, so those April reports and macro releases through May 15 are eligible. An observation about January–March is not automatically eligible in March: its publication date controls admission.

Use immutable dated BLS archives and BEA release PDFs, not current interactive series. The March 18 PPI release put February month-on-month growth at **0.7%**. The May 13 release revised February to **0.6%**. The pre-quarter packet deliberately retains 0.7%; the May vintage may acknowledge the newer revision but must not overwrite the earlier information set. BEA's webpage modification timestamp is not the economic release date; the preserved release PDF identifies the publication date. [March PPI release](https://www.bls.gov/news.release/archives/ppi_03182026.htm), [May PPI release](https://www.bls.gov/news.release/archives/ppi_05132026.htm).

No target-period company results were opened. Search snippets and a Marsh navigation index incidentally displayed later publications. They are excluded from the structured evidence and assumptions. This record does not claim that model pretraining or incidental exposure can be erased. Downstream scenario agents should receive only the eligible JSON records, not browsing transcripts.

## P&G: demand, pricing and costs

At the March cutoff, management's fiscal-year organic-sales range was 0–4%, commodity costs were expected to be neutral and tariffs were a $400m after-tax headwind. These are annual management expectations, not realized Q4 costs. January real consumption growth of 0.1% and February inflation provide reasons to avoid assuming strong volume simply because nominal spending rose. Broad producer prices are an imperfect proxy for P&G's procurement basket. [P&G January guidance](https://www.pginvestor.com/news/news-details/2026/PG-Announces-Fiscal-Year-2026-Second-Quarter-Results/default.aspx), [BEA January release](https://www.bea.gov/sites/default/files/2026-03/pi0126.pdf).

By May, P&G's commodity outlook had moved to a $150m annual after-tax headwind and management expected EPS toward the lower end of its range. The inflation releases corroborate cost pressure, while March real consumption still grew much less than nominal consumption. A defensible response is to widen the downside for input costs and household demand, allowing procurement, inventory and retail price lags. The annual $150m should not automatically become a Q4 charge: the model needs already-recognized costs and timing information. [P&G April guidance](https://www.pginvestor.com/news/news-details/2026/PG-Announces-Fiscal-Year-2026-Third-Quarter-Results/default.aspx), [BEA March release](https://www.bea.gov/sites/default/files/2026-04/pi0326.pdf).

Competition is not unambiguously adverse. Unilever's Q1 volume-led growth indicates category resilience, but its Home Care volume growth alongside slightly negative pricing also supports a competitive-price scenario. Different product and geographic mixes prevent interpreting its performance as P&G market-share loss. [Unilever April 30 release](https://www.unilever.com/files/unilever-q1-2026-full-announcement.pdf).

The factual CPI/PPI observations bind the evidence register. Their mapping into P&G's realized price/mix, unit growth, COGS or selling costs does not. Do not invent unit sales, price per unit, staff costs or procurement weights. Use disclosed growth components or explicitly labeled indices.

## Travelers: pricing, losses and investments

Travelers reported Business Insurance renewal premium change of 6.1% in Q4 2025, falling to 5.8% in Q1 2026, while retention rose from 85% to 86%. Renewal premium change includes exposure as well as rate; it is not pure price and is not total written-premium growth. Marsh's dated global commercial rate decline is an external competition signal, but its regional and product mix differs from Travelers. The primary dated Marsh index was verified. The linked release initially timed out but was subsequently downloaded. Its body carries an inconsistent 2025 date label despite describing Q4 2025; publication eligibility relies on the dated February 11, 2026 primary index. This discrepancy keeps source confidence at medium. [Travelers January release](https://investor.travelers.com/newsroom/press-releases/news-details/2026/Travelers-Reports-Excellent-Fourth-Quarter-and-Full-Year-Results/default.aspx), [Travelers April release](https://investor.travelers.com/newsroom/press-releases/news-details/2026/Travelers-Reports-Excellent-First-Quarter-Results/default.aspx), [Marsh Q4 release](https://www.marsh.com/ph/about/media/global-commercial-insurance-rates-fall-4-percent-in-q4-2025.html).

Written premiums measure business booked; earned premiums recognize coverage supplied over the policy term. April–June earnings therefore depend on earlier writings and unearned-premium runoff as well as current renewals. A pricing change cannot be applied immediately to every dollar of earned premium. Model the written-to-earned bridge where historical data supports it; otherwise disclose an illustrative lag. The Canada disposal also requires a consistent comparison perimeter.

Social inflation is a separate long-tail liability issue from consumer-price inflation. Swiss Re's older structural estimate supports a wider adverse-severity scenario, not a 7% surcharge on all Travelers claims. Travelers' May injury report further illustrates why declining injury frequency need not mean lower severity. Its multi-year sample is largely already embedded in historical experience; use it as a challenge to overly optimistic assumptions. [Swiss Re study](https://www.swissre.com/dam/jcr%3A6bc7d3b7-0f42-4209-a01a-e22787b98685/sri-sigma4-2024-litigation-costs-claims-inflation-final.pdf), [Travelers injury report](https://investor.travelers.com/newsroom/press-releases/news-details/2026/Travelers-Injury-Impact-Report-Highlights-Longer-Recovery-Times-Amid-Declining-Injury-Rates/default.aspx).

Underlying claims, underwriting expenses, catastrophes and prior-year reserve development must stay separate. An underlying combined ratio combines losses and expenses; do not label it a claims-only ratio. Favorable reserve releases are not guaranteed recurring revenue. NOAA's seasonal drought outlook is prospective hazard information, not an insured-loss estimate. It cannot identify Travelers' exposures, coverage, deductibles or reinsurance recovery. The evidence supports retaining a broad catastrophe stress range, with no weather-calibrated central estimate. [NOAA March 20 outlook](https://prod-01-alb-www-noaa.woc.noaa.gov/news-release/spring-outlook-drought-forecasted-to-expand-in-us-west-parts-of-plains).

## Rates, equity and attribution

The Fed held its target at 3.50–3.75% in March and April. A stable policy rate does not mean constant bond yields, credit spreads or company borrowing costs. For P&G, distinguish debt repricing from the equity discount rate. For Travelers, coupon income changes gradually as cash is invested and securities mature. Apply yield sensitivity to the reinvested fraction, and separately model invested-asset growth. A yield rise can lower bond fair values and equity through accumulated other comprehensive income while improving later reinvestment income; its impact on equity return is not identical to its impact on quarterly investment income. Equity market gains/losses should not be inferred from the Fed target alone. [March Fed statement](https://www.federalreserve.gov/newsevents/pressreleases/monetary20260318a.htm), [April Fed statement](https://www.federalreserve.gov/newsevents/pressreleases/monetary20260429a.htm).

To isolate market research, compare four runs: old-history only; old-history plus pre-quarter evidence; refreshed-history only; refreshed-history plus May evidence. The change from old to refreshed historical control is a history refresh, not research alpha. Within the evidence adjustment, select one combined household-demand stress and one combined input-cost stress for P&G; select a combined severity stress for Travelers. Do not simply sum every row's range. Management cost guidance, CPI, PPI and peer evidence often describe overlapping shocks.

## Structured handoff

`market_evidence.json` is authoritative; CSV is a flat equivalent. Filter by `company`, publication cutoff, and `excluded_from_model == false` before giving records to an agent. Every suggested adjustment is a range relative to the matching historical control, not an absolute forecast. Percent changes in income, percentage-point growth adjustments and basis-point margin changes have different units. Facts, agency forecasts and management guidance are explicitly typed. The arithmetic engine must reject post-cutoff publication dates and must not treat a published forecast as an observed target outcome.
