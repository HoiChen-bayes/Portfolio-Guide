# Case-study questions and analysis plan

## P&G: demand, pricing, costs and cash

1. How much target-quarter revenue growth is supported by company history and guidance, and how much depends on discretionary demand or price/mix assumptions?
2. Does stronger nominal consumer spending represent real demand, or mostly inflation? How does peer volume-led growth challenge a pricing-led forecast?
3. How should commodity, freight and tariff pressure affect gross margin after procurement and inventory lags, without double counting management's annual guidance?
4. Can selling and administrative investment support demand without assuming immediate productivity benefits?
5. How do non-operating items, effective tax, minority interests and diluted shares convert operating performance into EPS?
6. What operating-cash conversion and capital-spending assumptions are needed for cash generation, and are they disclosed observations or modeled proxies?

The implemented P&G contract uses sales growth, gross margin, SG&A ratio, net non-operating income, effective tax rate, noncontrolling income, diluted shares, operating-cash-flow/net-income conversion and capex/sales. Unit and price components can be discussed as explanatory indices where undisclosed; do not fabricate units or average selling prices. Separate nonrecurring items and GAAP/non-GAAP measures.

First establish the period-aligned historical controls. Then examine one combined demand adjustment and one combined cost adjustment from the eligible research. Translate them into coherent bear/base/bull assumptions. Calculate statements deterministically, reconcile earnings and cash, freeze assumptions, then compare with held-out actuals. Attribute differences to historical refresh, research, and scenario selection separately. Annual guidance is a reasonableness check; allocating its entire cost to Q4 is not justified.

## Travelers: premiums, underwriting risk and investment income

1. How do renewal rate, exposure, retention and new business translate into written premiums, and when will those premiums be earned?
2. What changes in business mix or divestitures distort the year-on-year comparison?
3. Does the underlying combined ratio reflect claims severity, frequency or expenses, and which elements cannot be separated from public information?
4. How wide should catastrophe scenarios remain when weather outlooks cannot identify insured exposure, event loss or reinsurance recoveries?
5. How much favorable prior-year reserve development is appropriate to assume, without treating it as guaranteed recurring profit?
6. How do invested assets, coupon/reinvestment lag, taxes and diluted shares convert underwriting results into net income and EPS?

The implemented Travelers contract uses earned-premium growth, written-premium growth, underlying combined ratio, catastrophe losses, favorable reserve development, investment income, other income including interest, effective tax rate and diluted shares. These are insurance drivers; retail COGS, selling price per unit and inventory-turn models are inappropriate substitutes.

Build written and earned growth separately, retaining an explicit timing limitation if an earning bridge cannot be calibrated. Model underlying underwriting results before adding catastrophes and reserve development. Apply broad economic and social-inflation evidence once through a combined severity challenge. Add investment income separately with yield lag. A policy-rate change cannot reprice the entire bond portfolio immediately; bond valuation and equity effects differ from income effects. Freeze assumptions before opening target actuals, then evaluate premium, underwriting, catastrophe, investment and EPS errors separately.

## Shared decision criteria

Prefer a transparent miss over an apparently accurate result obtained using later information. Report absolute and relative forecast errors with clear units, the bear/base/bull interval and whether actuals fell inside it. Do not claim statistically validated predictive superiority from two companies and one target quarter. An observed improvement after research is descriptive; larger holdout samples would be needed to establish generalizable forecast value.
