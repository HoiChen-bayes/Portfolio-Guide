# Discount policy review

**Decision requested:** Authorise design of a limited pricing test, subject to contract and demand checks. No rollout approval or realised saving is claimed.

**Scope:** Microsoft Financial Sample, 2014 Paseo / Small Business. Figures are sample currency units. This is an illustrative proposal built from historical data, not a live company recommendation. Profit means net sales less COGS.

## Economics

The historical scope generated sales of **10,557,376.50** and sample profit of **1,118,626.50** on 37,755 units. Gross unit price was 300 and average unit COGS was 250.

Reducing the effective discount rate by **2 percentage points** raises net unit price by 6. With a **5% volume decline**, modelled profit becomes **1,277,898.68**, an increase of **159,272.18** before implementation costs. With a **25% decline**, profit falls to **1,008,867.38**, below baseline by **109,759.12**. The volume assumptions are illustrative and have not been estimated from customer behaviour.

## Decision limits

Under unchanged gross price and unit COGS, a volume decline of approximately **16.8%** eliminates the modelled profit improvement before implementation costs. The 5% decline scenario therefore has room for at most **159,272.18** of incremental implementation expense across the full historical scope before its benefit reaches zero. This is a mathematical ceiling, not an approved spending budget or a small-pilot budget. A smaller test needs its own volume, duration and cost base.

Fixed overhead, returns, credit losses, tax, cash timing and capacity effects are absent. Any incremental expense reduces the modelled benefit one for one. The 16.8% boundary would become stricter once such expense is included. A positive profit case does not establish positive cash flow.

## Evidence needed before a test

| Proposed role | Evidence requested | Why it matters | Status |
|---|---|---|---|
| Sales / commercial lead | Contract price floors, promotion commitments, customer-level volumes and price history | Establish eligibility and credible demand response | Not obtained |
| Cost accounting | Product costs, freight, rebates, returns and cost allocation | Test whether unit COGS stays constant | Not obtained |
| Operations | Fulfilment capacity and service constraints | Check whether demand changes affect unit cost or service | Not obtained |
| Finance | Test implementation cost, collection terms and incremental working capital | Convert sample contribution into fuller economic and cash assessment | Not obtained |

## Proposed test design

After confirming eligibility, define comparable test and control groups, a fixed test window, a pre-agreed discount change and a minimum commercially meaningful result. Customer/order data are required; this dataset cannot create a credible control group. Compare realised net unit price, volume, total contribution, returns and service performance with the control, allowing for product mix and seasonality. Set the sample size and decision threshold using historical variability before running the test.

Stop or review if contract constraints are breached, service worsens, or observed contribution after incremental test expense fails the agreed criterion. Do not use a before/after sales change alone as proof that the discount policy caused the outcome.

**Current recommendation:** retain the current policy while collecting evidence. Prepare a bounded test for approval. The analysis supports further investigation, not immediate rollout.
